# SaleKhoj on TikTok/Instagram — a plan, not a fantasy

Researched 2026-08-04. Sources cited inline; anything not sourced is a stated guess, flagged as such.

## Reality check

Nepal has roughly 14.8M social-media user identities (~50% of population) and 18.4M Facebook users as of mid-2026 — Facebook, YouTube, and TikTok are the three most-used platforms, in that order ([DataReportal Digital 2026: Nepal](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-nepal), [NapoleonCat Facebook Nepal](https://stats.napoleoncat.com/facebook-users-in-nepal/)). That's the entire addressable audience — not a niche. But "deal content" as a category in Nepal is thin and mostly informal: search results for Nepali deal/shopping accounts return boutique resellers posting product photos with WhatsApp/Instagram DM ordering, not organized deal-aggregation content ([TikTok #daraznepal tag](https://www.tiktok.com/tag/daraznepal?lang=en)). There is no incumbent "Nepali dealsfinder" account with a large following that this research surfaced — which is either a gap worth taking, or evidence the format doesn't travel here (see "What could make this fail"). SaleKhoj's real asset isn't reach, it's supply: 10,876 verified two-price deals updated by a script, which is more structured deal inventory than any single Nepali reseller account has, and it costs zero marginal effort per post to source content from data that already exists.

## Platform priority for Nepal

1. **Instagram Reels first.** Reels get algorithmic distribution to non-followers similar to TikTok, Nepal's Instagram base is large and shopping-literate (influencer marketing platforms like MeroClub and Daraz's own affiliate program already route through Instagram — [Daraz Affiliate Marketing in Nepal](https://creativezinc.com/daraz-affiliate-marketing-in-nepal/)), and Instagram gives 5 link slots in bio plus Story swipe-up/link stickers, which matters a lot for a link-out business (see traffic routing below). Instagram is also not politically exposed the way TikTok has been.
2. **TikTok second.** TikTok is legal and unbanned in Nepal as of August 2024 and remains so through 2026 ([Kathmandu Post, Nepal lifts its ban on TikTok](https://kathmandupost.com/national/2024/08/23/nepal-lifts-its-ban-on-tiktok), [Wikipedia: TikTok ban in Nepal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TikTok_ban_in_Nepal)), and it is one of the three most-used apps in the country ([DataReportal](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-nepal)). But it carries residual risk (it was banned once already, for "social harmony" reasons, and the government now requires TikTok to promote tourism/education), and — critically — **Nepal is not on TikTok's Creator Rewards Program country list**, so there's no platform payout, only brand-deal/affiliate income ([TikTok Creator Fund Countries 2026](https://ttcalculator.net/data/reference/creator-fund-countries/)). Post here for reach, not for platform monetization.
3. **Facebook Reels third, don't skip it.** Facebook is still Nepal's single biggest platform by users (18.4M) and skews older/family-shopper, which may match SaleKhoj's median deal price point (Rs 2,799) better than TikTok's younger skew. Cross-post the same Reel/video asset — near-zero extra cost once it exists.

## Content formats (with real SaleKhoj data)

Formats ranked by production cost, cheapest first, since that's the actual constraint here.

**1. Screen-recorded scroll ("watch me find this deal").** Screen-record scrolling the live site filtering to a vertical, landing on a deal, zooming the price. Zero editing skill needed — this is a category proven to work broadly for South Asian deal/coupon accounts (e.g., Indian "loot deal" Telegram-to-Reel accounts use raw screen capture). Cost: near-zero, ~2 min per clip.
- Example: filter Beauty → Bucket "Toner" → land on **obsessioncosmetics.com — ANUA HEARTLEAF 77% SOOTHING TONER 250ml, Rs 490 (was Rs 3,200, 84% off)**. Caption: "Rs 490 toner that was Rs 3,200 last week 👀 link in bio."

**2. Faceless slideshow / carousel, one deal per slide.** 5-7 slides: product photo (from `data.json`'s `image` field), brand, struck-through original price, current price, discount badge, CTA slide. This is the dominant faceless format for deal/price content in 2026 per the commercial tooling landscape (ReelMoney, ShortsFaceless, PassiveShorts all center on exactly this image-carousel-plus-caption pattern — [ReelMoney](https://reel.money/), [ShortsFaceless](https://www.shortsfaceless.com/)). Works as both a static IG carousel post (no video encoding at all) and a Ken-Burns-panned video for Reels/TikTok.
- Example set (real 85%+ discounts across verticals): tudoholic.com Five-Quarter Sleeve Dress Rs 399 (was Rs 2,799, 85% off); obsessioncosmetics.com COSRX Hyaluronic Acid Essence Rs 490 (was Rs 2,900, 83% off); brother-mart.com Recci Space Capsule Wireless Mouse Rs 2,499 (was Rs 12,999, 80% off); tudoholic.com Seamless Fitness Briefs Rs 399 (was Rs 1,799, 77% off). Title card: "4 deals over 75% off in Nepal right now."

**3. "Top 5 [vertical] deals this week" countdown video.** Same slideshow mechanics as #2 but themed and cadenced weekly, which gives a reason to follow (recurring format = habit). Pull top-N by `discount_pct` per vertical straight from `data.json` — this is literally one `sort()` call on data that already exists.

**4. Price-drop / "this actually happened" hook.** Lead with the shock number, not the product: open on "Rs 12,999 → Rs 2,499" text animation before revealing what it is. Hook-first framing is the single most consistent lesson from short-form deal/commerce content generally (attention within first 1-2s decides completion rate) — this is a widely-repeated platform best practice rather than something I have a Nepal-specific citation for; flagging as inference, not sourced fact.

**5. (Stretch, skip initially) Talking-head "I checked if this deal is real" review.** Highest production cost, highest trust payoff, but requires an on-camera person, contradicts the "no backend, minimal" ethos, and isn't needed for launch. Revisit only if faceless formats plateau.

## Technical pipeline — the laziest thing that works

The whole point of SaleKhoj is a static site with no backend; the content pipeline should match that ethos: a script that reads `site/data.json` and writes video/image files, no SaaS.

- **What generates it:** a single Python script (`scripts/social.py` or similar, sibling to `scrapers/`) that:
  1. Reads `site/data.json`, filters/sorts by vertical + `discount_pct` (data already has everything needed: `brand`, `title`, `image`, `price`, `original_price`, `discount_pct`).
  2. Downloads each product's `image` if not cached.
  3. Uses **Pillow** to composite each image with price/discount text onto a fixed-size (1080×1920) canvas — one PNG per slide. No video needed for the IG-carousel format at all (a carousel post is just N images).
  4. For the video (Reels/TikTok) version: **ffmpeg** concatenates the PNGs with a slide duration + crossfade (`xfade` filter) and a background audio track (royalty-free, downloaded once, reused), output H.264 1080×1920 MP4. This is a ~40-60 line ffmpeg invocation, not Remotion — Remotion means a Node/React/Chromium render pipeline for a task ffmpeg already does in one process ([FFmpeg slideshow guide](https://www.ffmpeg-micro.com/blog/ffmpeg-create-slideshow-from-images)).
  5. Script prints file paths; posting itself is **manual** at launch (no auto-poster, no scheduler dependency, no TikTok/IG API app review to go through) — open the folder, upload by hand. Automate posting only after format/cadence is proven to be worth automating.
- **What runs it, how often:** run on WSL2 as part of (or right after) `./build.sh`, since it already has fresh `data.json`. Weekly cadence for the "top 5" format is enough at launch — one script run produces a week's worth of slide decks in one pass.
- **Effort estimate:** the Pillow compositor + ffmpeg concat script is a half-day to a day of work for someone who knows the codebase already (it's the same shape as `extract.py` — read JSON, transform, write files). No new infra, no new dependency beyond `Pillow` and the `ffmpeg` binary (WSL2 apt package). This is the "shortest working diff" — skip Remotion, skip any paid faceless-video SaaS, skip auto-posting until it's proven necessary.

## Posting cadence and 90-day expectation

- **Cadence:** 3-4 Reels/week on Instagram (mirrored to TikTok and FB Reels same-day), no more — quality-of-hook matters more than volume for an account with zero existing audience, and 3-4/week is sustainable from a once-weekly batch-generation run.
- **90-day numbers, stated as an estimate with the assumptions shown, not a promise:** starting from zero followers with no paid boost, no known Nepali deal-content precedent to benchmark against directly (this research found no comparable large Nepali dealsfinder account to calibrate off), a realistic new-account trajectory on Reels/TikTok in a market of ~15M social users is **low hundreds to low thousands of followers by day 90**, with individual Reels occasionally spiking into the tens of thousands of views if a specific deal (e.g., an 80%+ off item) resonates — spiky, not linear, growth is the normal pattern for deal/commerce accounts because reach is driven by the discount number in the hook, not by subscriber count. Revenue from platform funds: **$0**, because Nepal isn't on TikTok's Creator Rewards country list ([ttcalculator.net](https://ttcalculator.net/data/reference/creator-fund-countries/)) and Instagram has no equivalent bonus program active in Nepal. Any monetization in 90 days would have to come from affiliate commissions if SaleKhoj adds them (currently explicitly "none yet, by decision" per CLAUDE.md) or from brand sponsorship once follower count clears roughly the 10K micro-influencer threshold, where Nepali campaigns reportedly pay NPR 5,000-20,000 ([Creative Zinc](https://creativezinc.com/daraz-affiliate-marketing-in-nepal/)) — unlikely inside 90 days from zero.

## Monetization and traffic routing (for context, not an ask to build now)

- Nepali influencer pay bands reported: micro (1K-10K) NPR 5,000-20,000/campaign, mid (10K-100K) NPR 15,000-75,000, large (100K+) NPR 75,000-250,000 per campaign ([Creative Zinc](https://creativezinc.com/daraz-affiliate-marketing-in-nepal/)) — treat this as directional, not verified against a primary rate card.
- TikTok's Creator Rewards Program does **not** pay out in Nepal; brand deals and affiliate links are the only in-country monetization paths ([ttcalculator.net](https://ttcalculator.net/data/reference/creator-fund-countries/)).
- **Link routing:** TikTok limits personal (non-Shop, non-Business) accounts to a single external link; Instagram allows up to 5 links but shows one at a time. The standard workaround is a link-in-bio page (Linktree-style) pointing to the SaleKhoj deals page or a specific vertical filter URL ([Jotform: Linktree alternatives 2026](https://www.jotform.com/blog/linktree-alternatives/)). Given the minimal-infra ethos, the cheapest version is a static one-page HTML file hosted alongside `site/` (no third-party link-in-bio SaaS needed) linking to 3-5 of the site's own URLs (home, this week's top deals, a vertical page) — consistent with "no backend."

## What could make this fail

- **No evidence anyone in Nepal wants "aggregated deal" content specifically** — this search found individual boutique/reseller accounts, not deal-aggregator accounts, meaning the format may simply not be a habit Nepali shoppers have (they may prefer following a store directly over following a deal-curator).
- **Zero platform monetization in Nepal** removes the algorithm's usual incentive alignment (TikTok doesn't need to reward Nepali creators to keep them posting), so growth depends entirely on organic reach, which is the least controllable variable.
- **Data freshness risk translates to embarrassment risk on video**, unlike on the site: CLAUDE.md notes Nepali store uptime is flaky (a site can show 718 deals one run and 0 the next); posting a video around a deal that's gone dead or reverted price by the time someone clicks through burns trust faster on social than a quietly-stale website row does.
- **The `spread()`/`bucket` logic that keeps the homepage balanced doesn't automatically prevent one store dominating the social feed** — the compositor script needs the same 2-per-store discipline the site already has (`spread()` in `app.js`), or a handful of the biggest-discount stores (tudoholic.com, obsessioncosmetics.com, brother-mart.com are the top discounters in this data) will visually own every post.
- **TikTok's ban history in Nepal is a live tail risk**, not a settled fact — it was banned once for vague "social harmony" reasons and could be again; don't build anything TikTok-exclusive.

Skipped: auto-posting/scheduling APIs, paid faceless-video SaaS, Remotion/React video rendering, affiliate-link infrastructure (site has none yet by explicit decision) — add scheduling only once posting cadence is proven worth automating; add affiliate infra only if/when the site itself adds affiliate links.
